Do we really want a global energy war and an Arms Race?

By Ankit Gupta | Thusday, 19 March, 2026 | Human Envisaged, LLM Researched, LLM Drafted, Human Cross-checked, Human Edited

Q1: Can we endorse deals between 2 countries that can potentially have global repercussions?

Q2: A new global order which is same as the old one, is it even term-able as progress?

Q3: Can a global leader be a leader if he / she acts in interest of just a few?

I asked an LLM which was trained on updated data only till October 2023 to give its analysis on the global geopolitical events I listed as a scenario that might happen, following is its unedited response.

If Cuba and/or Greenland are considered steps 5 and 6 of an initial strategy (https://www.linkedin.com/posts/datascientistankit_i-usually-refrain-from-commenting-on-war-activity-7434727702957416448-I7LZ?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop&rcm=ACoAABwUnAUB5nRWZ2uCpeNcnWFddHo2OUal1tE) that involves a sequence of geopolitical and military actions, it suggests a broader and more comprehensive plan aimed at reshaping the global order in favor of the United States. Let's break down the potential sequence of events, the rationale behind including Cuba and Greenland, and the implications for the U.S. and the world.

Initial Strategy Sequence

1. Military Action in the Caspian Region (Russia and Ukraine):

U.S. Support for Ukraine: Provide military aid, intelligence, and diplomatic support to Ukraine to resist Russian aggression and disrupt Russian energy supplies.

Impact: Disruption of energy supplies to Europe and Asia, increased military presence, and potential conflicts in the region.

2. Military Action in the Persian Gulf (Israel and Palestine):

U.S. Support for Israel: Provide military aid, intelligence, and diplomatic support to Israel to assert its influence in the Persian Gulf.

Impact: While there might be some military action, U.S. support could help prevent significant energy disruptions in the Persian Gulf.

3. Military Intervention in Venezuela:

U.S. Intervention: Intervene in Venezuela to secure its oil reserves, further disrupting global energy supplies.

Impact: Further impact on global energy markets and prices.

4. Military Action in the Middle East (Led by the U.S. and Continued by Israel):

U.S. and Israel Joint Action: Lead a military intervention in the Middle East, with Israel continuing the action, further disrupting oil supplies and asserting control over critical energy infrastructure.

Impact: Exacerbation of global energy shortages and price volatility.

5. Military Action in Cuba:

U.S. Intervention: Launch a military intervention in Cuba to assert control over the island, secure strategic locations, and counter the influence of adversaries like Russia and China.

Rationale: Cuba's strategic location in the Caribbean makes it a key point for controlling maritime routes and monitoring activities in the region. Asserting control over Cuba could enhance U.S. influence in Latin America and the Caribbean.

6. Military Action in Greenland:

U.S. Intervention: Conduct a military intervention in Greenland to secure control over key strategic locations and natural resources, with or without Danish cooperation.

Rationale: Greenland's strategic location in the Arctic makes it a critical point for monitoring and controlling maritime routes, which are becoming increasingly important due to climate change. Asserting control over Greenland could enhance U.S. influence in the Arctic and secure access to valuable natural resources.

Implications for the U.S.

1. Geopolitical Influence:

Enhanced Influence: Successful implementation of this strategy could significantly enhance U.S. geopolitical influence in key regions, including Eastern Europe, the Middle East, Latin America, and the Arctic.

Global Order: The U.S. could reshape the global order in its favor, asserting control over critical resources and strategic locations.

2. Energy Security:

Reduced Dependence: The U.S. could reduce its dependence on foreign energy sources, enhancing energy security and resilience.

Control Over Infrastructure: Gaining control over critical energy infrastructure in disrupted regions could ensure long-term energy security.

3. Economic Impact:

Higher Energy Prices: The U.S. could benefit from higher energy prices due to supply disruptions, enhancing its economic position.

Increased Control: The U.S. could gain increased control over global energy markets, positioning itself as a key energy provider.

Implications for the World

1. Geopolitical Tensions:

Increased Tensions: The world would likely see a significant increase in geopolitical tensions, with potential for broader conflicts and alliances shifting.

Regional Instability: Both Latin America, the Caribbean, and the Arctic region could experience heightened instability, with potential spillover effects in neighboring countries.

2. International Relations:

Diplomatic Isolation: The U.S. could face diplomatic isolation, with many countries and international organizations condemning its actions. This could lead to a realignment of global alliances.

United Nations and NATO: The United Nations and NATO could impose sanctions or other punitive measures on the U.S., further isolating it on the global stage.

3. Military Escalation:

Potential Conflicts: The actions could lead to military escalation, with potential for broader conflicts involving Russia, China, and other regional powers.

Arms Race: There could be an arms race, with countries increasing their military spending and capabilities in response to U.S. actions.

4. Economic Impact:

Global Economy: The global economy could be significantly impacted, with increased energy prices, supply chain disruptions, and economic instability.

Trade and Investment: There could be shifts in global trade and investment patterns, with countries seeking to secure alternative energy sources and diversify their supply chains.

5. Humanitarian Crisis:

Cuba and Greenland: The populations of Ukraine, Iran, Cuba and Greenland could face humanitarian crises, with potential for significant casualties, displacement, and economic hardship.

Conclusion

Including Cuba and Greenland as steps 5 and 6 of an initial strategy suggests a comprehensive and ambitious plan aimed at reshaping the global order in favor of the United States. While this strategy could lead to significant geopolitical gains, energy security, and economic benefits for the U.S., it also carries substantial risks, including increased geopolitical tensions, diplomatic isolation, military escalation, economic impact, and potential humanitarian crises. The success or failure of this strategy would depend on careful planning, diplomatic outreach, and preparation for potential escalation, as well as the willingness of the U.S. to bear the significant risks and consequences. The character of U.S. leadership in this scenario would be perceived as aggressive, assertive, strategic, and controversial, with long-term implications for U.S. influence, the global order, and domestic politics.
